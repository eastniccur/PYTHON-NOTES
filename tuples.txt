Tuples are almost identical to lists, but with one massive rule: 
-->they are immutable (locked in stone). Once created, you cannot .append(), .insert(), or .pop() items.
Why use tuples if they are locked?

1.Speed & Memory: Python processes tuples faster and uses less RAM.

2.Data Safety: Prevents accidental modification of constant data (like GPS coordinates or configuration settings).

To modify a tuple, you must convert it to a list using list(), change it, and convert it back using tuple().
